# Express Entry Canada: A Complete Beginner's Guide

If you are researching how to become a Canadian permanent resident, you have probably come across **Express Entry**. It is one of Canada's main systems for managing applications from skilled workers.

Express Entry can seem complicated at first because it involves different immigration programs, language tests, work experience, education assessments and a points-based ranking system. This guide explains the process in plain language, from checking whether you qualify to submitting a permanent residence application.

**Important:** Immigration rules and selection priorities can change. This guide reflects the official information available on November 14, 2026. Always check the linked Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) pages before making a decision or submitting an application. This article is general information, not legal advice.

## What is Express Entry?

Express Entry is an online system used by IRCC to manage applications for permanent residence under three federal economic immigration programs:

1. **Canadian Experience Class (CEC):** for skilled workers with qualifying Canadian work experience.
2. **Federal Skilled Worker Program (FSWP):** for skilled workers with qualifying work experience in Canada or abroad.
3. **Federal Skilled Trades Program (FSTP):** for people qualified in eligible skilled trades.

Express Entry is not a separate visa or a single immigration program. It is the system through which eligible candidates create a profile, enter a pool, receive a ranking and may be invited to apply for permanent residence.

Official overview: [IRCC — Immigrate through Express Entry](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry.html).

## The three programs explained

### 1. Canadian Experience Class (CEC)

CEC is designed for people who have gained qualifying skilled work experience in Canada.

In general, the experience must:
- Be in an eligible National Occupational Classification (NOC) TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation.
- Include the actions in the NOC lead statement and most of its main duties.
- Be paid work.
- Add up to at least one year, or 1,560 hours, in the three years before applying.
- Have been gained while you were authorized to work in Canada.

Some work does not count, including work completed while you were a full-time student for the purpose of meeting the CEC minimum, unpaid work and most self-employment. There are specific exceptions and detailed rules, so check IRCC's current instructions.

CEC has minimum language requirements that depend on the TEER category of the job. There is no education requirement for the program, although education can help improve a candidate's CRS ranking.

Read the official [Canadian Experience Class requirements](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/who-can-apply/canadian-experience-class.html).

### 2. Federal Skilled Worker Program (FSWP)

FSWP is for skilled workers with qualifying work experience in Canada or abroad.

The main work-experience requirements generally include:
- Work in a NOC TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation.
- At least one year of continuous paid work, or 1,560 hours, in the same primary occupation.
- Experience gained within the 10 years before applying.
- Evidence that you performed the NOC lead statement and most of the main duties.

Applicants must also meet the program's language and education requirements. Foreign education generally needs an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) for immigration purposes. Applicants are assessed against a separate selection-factor grid and generally need at least 67 out of 100 points to qualify for the FSWP before entering the Express Entry pool.

That 67-point eligibility score is **not** the same as the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score used to rank candidates in the pool.

Read the official [Federal Skilled Worker Program requirements](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/who-can-apply/federal-skilled-workers.html).

### 3. Federal Skilled Trades Program (FSTP)

FSTP is for qualified workers in eligible skilled trades.

The work-experience requirement generally includes at least two years of paid work experience, or 3,120 hours, in an eligible skilled trade during the five years before applying. The experience must meet the program's occupation, duties and qualification rules.

Applicants must also have either:
- A valid offer of full-time employment for at least one year; or
- A certificate of qualification in the skilled trade issued by a Canadian provincial, territorial or federal authority.

Language requirements apply. There is no education requirement for the FSTP, although education may improve a candidate's CRS score.

Read the official [Federal Skilled Trades Program requirements](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/who-can-apply/federal-skilled-trades.html).

## Step 1: Check which program you may qualify for

Before creating a profile, review the minimum requirements for all three programs. Your work history, occupation, language results, education and other circumstances determine which program may fit.

Do not choose a NOC code based only on a job title. Compare your actual duties with the NOC lead statement and main duties. A job title can be different from the NOC title, and the duties are important.

You can start with IRCC's [eligibility questionnaire and program comparison](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/who-can-apply.html).

## Step 2: Take an approved language test

You must take an IRCC-approved language test and meet the minimum score for the program you are applying under. The test assesses four abilities:
- Reading
- Writing
- Listening
- Speaking

English and French tests use different scoring systems. IRCC converts accepted test results to Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) or Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens (NCLC) levels.

Book the correct test for immigration purposes and make sure the results are valid when you create your profile and when you submit your permanent residence application. Check IRCC's accepted tests and validity rules before booking.

Official information: [IRCC language testing for Express Entry](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html).

## Step 3: Get your education assessed

If you completed your education outside Canada, you may need an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) for the FSWP. An ECA can also help you receive CRS points for foreign education.

An ECA checks how your foreign credential compares with a Canadian credential. It does not guarantee admission to a Canadian college, university or regulated profession.

Use an organization designated by IRCC for immigration ECAs, and check how long the assessment remains valid.

Official information: [IRCC — Educational Credential Assessments](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/education-assessed.html).

## Step 4: Understand your CRS score

Once you are eligible and create an Express Entry profile, IRCC uses the **Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS)** to rank you against other candidates in the pool.

Your CRS score can be affected by factors such as:
- Age
- Education
- English and French language ability
- Canadian work experience
- Certain high-wage Canadian work experience or qualifying job-offer factors under the rules introduced in November 2026
- Spouse or common-law partner factors, where applicable
- Skill-transferability combinations
- Additional factors such as French-language ability, Canadian education, a sibling in Canada or a provincial nomination

A provincial or territorial nomination through an eligible Express Entry stream can add 600 CRS points. A nomination does not remove the need to meet the requirements of the relevant program and nomination stream.

**Important November 2026 update:** IRCC announced changes effective November 3, 2026 to how CRS points are awarded for Canadian work experience and valid job offers. The changes introduced a Labour Market Integration factor and removed the previous CRS points for job offers. IRCC also said it would recalculate existing profiles, which could take up to two weeks, and pause invitation rounds during that recalculation. The online profile system may not yet reflect every change, so follow IRCC's specific instructions when updating a profile.

Do not rely on an old CRS calculator or an online score estimate without checking the current official rules.

Use the [official IRCC CRS calculator](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/check-score.html) and review the [CRS criteria and current changes](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/check-score/crs-criteria.html).

## Step 5: Create your Express Entry profile

If you meet the minimum requirements of at least one program, you can create an Express Entry profile through your IRCC secure account.

You will be asked for information such as:
- Passport or travel document details
- Language test results
- Education and ECA details, if applicable
- Work history and NOC information
- Family details
- Job-offer information, if applicable
- Provincial nomination details, if applicable

Answer accurately and keep your information consistent with your supporting documents. Creating a profile does not mean you have applied for permanent residence, and it does not guarantee that you will receive an invitation.

If your circumstances change, update your profile promptly. Examples include a new language result, a change in work experience, a new educational credential or a change in family composition.

Official instructions: [IRCC — Create your Express Entry profile](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/submit-profile.html).

## Step 6: Understand invitation rounds

IRCC holds rounds of invitations and invites candidates based on the type of round and the applicable selection rules.

Common round types include:
- **General rounds:** candidates who meet the relevant program criteria may be considered.
- **Program-specific rounds:** invitations are issued to candidates eligible for a particular Express Entry program.
- **Category-based rounds:** candidates who meet the requirements of a selected category may be considered, such as French-language proficiency or specified occupations.

In a round, candidates are ranked and invitations are issued to those who meet the round's criteria and ranking threshold. A CRS cut-off from a previous round is not a promise or prediction of the next cut-off. The number of invitations and score can change.

IRCC publishes the results and tie-breaking rules on its [Rounds of invitations page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/rounds-invitations.html).

## Step 7: If you receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA)

An Invitation to Apply means IRCC is inviting you to submit a complete permanent residence application. It is not an approval.

Read the invitation and your account instructions carefully. IRCC generally gives invited candidates **60 days** to submit the application.

You may need documents such as:
- Identity and civil-status documents
- Language test results
- Education documents and ECA report, where applicable
- Employer reference letters and proof of work experience
- Police certificates
- Medical examination information
- Proof of funds, if required
- Other documents requested for your circumstances

The exact checklist depends on your application. Start collecting documents early, especially police certificates and employer letters. Do not submit inaccurate information or claim work experience you cannot document.

## Step 8: Submit the permanent residence application

Complete the online forms, upload the requested documents and pay the applicable fees before the deadline. Review every answer carefully before submitting.

After submission, IRCC assesses the application. Depending on your case, you may be asked to provide biometrics, attend or complete a medical examination, provide additional documents or respond to questions.

An ITA does not guarantee approval. IRCC checks eligibility, admissibility, the accuracy of the information in your profile and the supporting evidence.

## Do you need proof of funds?

Proof-of-funds rules depend on the program and your circumstances.

In general, proof of funds is required for the Federal Skilled Worker Program and Federal Skilled Trades Program. It is not required for Canadian Experience Class applicants. Certain applicants who are authorized to work in Canada and have a valid job offer may also be exempt.

The required amount depends on family size and is updated by IRCC. Do not use an old table from a blog or social-media post. Check the current [IRCC proof-of-funds table and exemptions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/proof-funds.html).

## What if your CRS score is not high enough?

There is no single score that guarantees an invitation. The cut-off depends on the type of round, the number of invitations, the pool and the rules in force for that round.

Depending on your circumstances, possible ways to improve your profile may include:
- Improving your English or French test results.
- Taking an approved test in the other official language.
- Completing additional eligible education and obtaining an ECA where needed.
- Gaining additional qualifying work experience.
- Exploring eligible provincial nomination streams.
- Checking whether you meet the requirements for a category-based round.

Never pay someone who promises a guaranteed invitation or a guaranteed CRS cut-off. Be cautious of fake job offers, purchased employment letters or claims that an LMIA automatically guarantees permanent residence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Express Entry the same as permanent residence?

No. Express Entry is the system used to manage applications under three federal economic immigration programs. You first need to qualify, enter the pool and receive an invitation before submitting a permanent residence application.

### Can I create an Express Entry profile without a job offer?

Yes, a job offer is not required for the Canadian Experience Class or Federal Skilled Worker Program. The Federal Skilled Trades Program has a job-offer or certificate-of-qualification requirement. Check the current program rules and the November 2026 changes to job-offer points.

### Does a high CRS score guarantee an invitation?

No. Invitations depend on the round type, eligibility, ranking, number of invitations and rules in force. A score that was sufficient in a past round may not be sufficient in a future one.

### Can I apply through Express Entry if I live outside Canada?

Yes. The Federal Skilled Worker Program and Federal Skilled Trades Program can be relevant to eligible applicants outside Canada. The Canadian Experience Class requires qualifying Canadian work experience.

### Can I include my spouse and children?

You must provide accurate information about your spouse or partner and dependent children in your profile and application, whether or not they will accompany you. Family composition can affect your CRS calculation, fees, documents and proof-of-funds requirements.

### How long does Express Entry take?

There is no single timeline for everyone. Time can depend on the program, application completeness, background checks, document requests and IRCC's workload. Check IRCC's current processing-time tool rather than relying on an old estimate.
